1
Foundation
Strategy & Identity
▾
🎯
The "Target 20" Company List
Research 20 companies by culture, recent news, and pain points — not just job openings. Use LinkedIn, Glassdoor, and earnings calls. The goal: walk in knowing their problems before they tell you.
🔑
Extract 10 Power Keywords
Analyze 5 target job descriptions. Pull the repeating technical and soft skills. These exact phrases must appear in your resume, LinkedIn headline, and summary — verbatim for ATS.
🔍
Digital Footprint Audit New
Google yourself. What does a recruiter see? Clean up old social posts, ensure your GitHub/portfolio is current, and lock privacy on personal accounts. You're a brand — audit it quarterly.
💡
LinkedIn as a Sales Page
Your headline = your value proposition, not your job title. Example: "Turning messy data pipelines into revenue clarity · Data Eng @ Scale". Set "Open to Work" visible to recruiters only if employed.
2
Storytelling
Your Story Bank
▾
📖
The STAR Matrix — 10 Stories Minimum
Write 10 stories: Situation → Task → Action → Result. Map to: conflict resolution, failure + lesson, major win, leading without authority, cross-functional collaboration, tight deadline, data-driven decision, customer rescue, mentorship, and one wildcard.
📊
Quantify Everything — No Fuzzy Language
Every story needs a number. "Reduced processing time by 40%" beats "improved efficiency." Use: percentages, dollar impact, time saved, team size, user count, revenue influenced. No number? Find a proxy metric.
🗂️
The Living "Brag File" New
Maintain a private running doc of wins, praise emails, metrics, and completed projects — updated monthly. This fuels your STAR stories, resume bullets, and performance reviews. Never scramble for examples again.
🪞
The "So What?" Mirror Test New
Read every resume bullet aloud. Ask: "So what? Why does this matter?" If you can't answer in one sentence, rewrite it. Bullets must connect your action to a business outcome, not just describe a task.
3
Documents
Resume & Portfolio Arsenal
▾
📄
Master Resume → Targeted Resume System
Keep a master doc with everything. For each application, "Save As" and cut to only what matches that JD. ATS rewards relevance density, not length. Target: 85%+ keyword match on key skills.
🌐
Portfolio / GitHub / Personal Site
For technical or creative roles, a live portfolio proves what a resume claims. One clean project with a README is more powerful than five bullet points. It also signals initiative and craft.
💰
Know Your Number — Compensation Research Critical
Before any conversation: research via Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, and Blind. Know your target, acceptable floor, and walk-away number. Never be the first to anchor without data. Silence is power.
👥
Reference Pre-Check — Warm Them Now
Contact 3 references before you need them. Brief them on the roles you're targeting so they can tailor their praise. A surprised reference gives a generic one. A prepped reference gives a specific, compelling one.
4
Network Intelligence
Access the Hidden Market
▾
🕵️
Identify the Actual Hiring Manager Critical
Don't just apply — find the person you'd report to on LinkedIn. Follow them. Engage genuinely with their content for 2–3 weeks before outreach. Warm ≠ cold. A name-drop in your cover letter changes everything.
☕
Network Activation, Not Cold Outreach
Instead of cold "coffee chat" requests, lead with value: share a relevant article, comment with insight, or ask a specific question on their post. Requests that give first convert at 3–5× higher rates.
🔗
Internal Referral Strategy New
A referred candidate is 10× more likely to be hired. Before applying, check if any 1st or 2nd-degree connection works there. A simple "Would you be comfortable referring me?" message can unlock the whole process.
📅
The 30-60-90 Day Plan
Draft a rough outline of your first 3 months in the role. Listen, learn, quick wins, then strategic impact. Mentioning this in interviews signals you're already thinking like an employee — not a candidate hoping to get lucky.
5
Interview Readiness
Own the Room (or the Screen)
▾
❓
5–7 Reverse Interview Questions
Bad: "What are the benefits?" — Good: "How does the team measure success in the first 90 days?" or "Where has this role struggled historically, and why?" Great questions signal strategic thinking.
🎥
Remote Setup Audit
Light in front of you, not behind. Eye-level camera. Clean or blurred background. USB or condenser mic — laptop mics sound desperate. Test 10 minutes before every interview, not the morning of.
🧠
Emotional & Mindset Prep New
Recruiters are trained to detect anxiety and desperation — both repel offers. Practice your stories aloud until they feel conversational, not rehearsed. Confidence isn't arrogance; it's preparation wearing a calm face.
📝
The 24-Hour Follow-Up System New
Send a specific, personalized thank-you within 24 hours — not "Thanks for your time." Reference one thing discussed, reaffirm your fit, and add a thought you didn't get to share. This alone separates 80% of candidates.
6
Offer Stage
Negotiate — Don't Just Accept
▾
⚖️
Never Accept on the Spot Critical
Always ask for 24–48 hours to review. Every offer is a starting point, not a final number. Saying "I'm very excited — can I have a day to review the full package?" has never cost anyone an offer. Silence after an offer is leverage.
📋
Negotiate the Full Package
Base is just one lever. Push on: signing bonus, remote flexibility, equity vesting cliff, title, start date, and professional development budget. Companies often have more flexibility here than on base when HR has budget constraints.
🤝
Keep All Pipelines Active Until Signed New
Never pause your search based on a verbal offer. Until the written, signed offer is in your hand, treat every other opportunity as still live. Competing offers — even implied ones — are the single most powerful negotiation tool you have.
★ Headhunter's Insider Edge
The candidates I place fastest aren't the most qualified — they're the most visible before the role opens. Build your presence on LinkedIn now: post one insight per week in your domain, engage with hiring managers at your Target 20 companies, and treat every DM as a long-term relationship, not a transaction. The best job offers arrive as conversations, not applications.
